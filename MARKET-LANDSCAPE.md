# MARKET-LANDSCAPE.md — binjreel

*The market record the platform design stands on. Researched 2026-07-28 by the Architect from
primary sources: ReelShort (reelshort.com), DramaBox, GoodShort, NetShort product listings and
teardowns, Sensor Tower's State of Short Drama reports (2025, 2026), Omdia/MPA figures as
reported by Variety, Rolling Stone, Hollywood Reporter, TechCrunch, and Filmustage's 2026
platform comparison. Figures are third-party estimates unless a company reported them directly;
treat exact numbers as directional, the shape as solid.*

---

## 1 — The market, in numbers

- Short-drama apps generated ~**$2.98B in in-app purchase revenue in 2025, up 115% YoY** — the
  third-fastest-growing app category Sensor Tower measures. Total market estimated ~**$14B**
  (heavily skewed by China's domestic ecosystem); forecast **$26B globally by 2030**.
- The **US contributes ~60% of global mobile revenue** — the single most monetizable market,
  which is binjreel's home market.
- Engagement beats Big Streaming on its own turf: **ReelShort US users average 35.7 min/day
  in-app** vs Netflix mobile 24.8, Prime Video 26.9, Disney+ 23 (Omdia, Q4 2025). Q4 2025
  microdrama downloads (733M) exceeded long-form streamers (658M).
- Category density: **13 short-drama apps sit in Google Play's Entertainment Top 50 Grossing**
  (26% of slots, May 2026) — up from 3–5 two years ago.
- The brutal part: **331 apps are live; the top 3 capture ~60% of non-China revenue; ~70% of
  active apps are expected to fold or be absorbed within two years.** Entering as app #332 with
  an undifferentiated romance catalog is a losing hand. Entering with a differentiated genre
  wedge and superior unit economics is the play the newest, best-funded entrants are making.

## 2 — The leaders

**ReelShort (Crazy Maple Studio / COL Group)** — the category creator for the West.
~**$1.2B gross consumer spend in 2025** (Appfigures), 55–70M MAU, all-original English-language
production in LA. Premium, brand-led — and **still loss-making**, burned by user-acquisition
spend. Part of a four-platform portfolio (FlareFlow, Sereal+, UniReel). Lesson: scale without
funnel efficiency is a treadmill.

**DramaBox (Singapore)** — the disciplined operator. **$323M revenue, $10M net profit in 2024**
(MPA) — the only profitable major. Best-in-class retention (17% at month six, 15% at month
twelve). Disney Accelerator alum; raising ~$100M at ~$500M valuation to expand in the US and
**diversify its genre slate**. First short-drama app with global programmatic ad inventory (The
Trade Desk, April 2026). Lesson: retention + a real ads business = profit at a third of the
leader's scale.

**NetShort** — the monetization machine. Youngest of the majors and has recently held the **#1
revenue spot month-over-month** (InsighTrackr, early 2026). The product is a rewards economy
wrapped around a catalog: daily check-ins, consecutive-login streaks, watch-ads-for-coins,
rewarded-ad episode unlocks, task center, VIP tiers with quality/download/ad-free perks,
episodes at 540p/720p/1080p, playback-speed control. Lesson: the free-earning economy is not a
discount program — it IS the engagement engine.

**GoodShort (NewReading)** — the quality-positioned mid-major. Cinematic production values;
notable product detail: **web top-up at "10% extra value vs the app"** — steering coin purchases
to the web where the store fee doesn't apply. Multi-language day-one (12+ languages), ENG-dubbed
shelves, tags as first-class navigation.

**The Hollywood wave (2025–26)** — the validation of binjreel's genre thesis:
- **MicroCo** (ex-WME/ABC, Showtime, NBCU chiefs): investing explicitly in genres beyond
  romance — true crime, thrillers, horror, anime, **comedy**.
- **Crisp** (Adrian Cheng): positioning beyond melodrama into **animation, bite-sized
  documentaries, creator-driven micro-series**.
- **GammaTime** (ex-Miramax CEO; investors incl. Alexis Ohanian, Kim Kardashian): ~$14M seed
  for premium verticals.
- **vertTV + a second studio launched the same day (July 2026)**: both explicitly pushing into
  action, thriller, **comedy**, crime, reality.
- **Mansa**: culturally-specific vertical micro-dramas inside an existing streamer.
- **SAG-AFTRA** is drawing up terms for verticals — the format is being institutionalized.

## 3 — The standard monetization stack (what "table stakes" means)

Every leader runs a **three-legged stool**; no major runs fewer than three:

1. **Coins per episode.** First 5–10 episodes free; remaining 40–90 episodes unlock at a
   per-episode coin price. A full series effectively costs **$15–50** to a paying viewer.
   Coin packs tier from ~$4.99/500 coins with bonus coins on larger packs.
2. **Subscription (VIP).** Weekly/annual unlimited tiers; perks beyond access: **ad-free,
   HD/1080p, offline download, early release**. NetShort's weekly sits at premium pricing with
   a trial; annual plans reward the committed binger.
3. **The free-earning economy.** Daily check-in (~10 coins, streak escalation), rewarded ads
   (5–30s, capped ~15–20/day, ~250 coins/day achievable), watch-time and task rewards,
   referral bonuses, one-time bonuses (link email, etc.), and **rewarded-ad episode unlocks**
   (watch one ad → this episode plays). Earned coins **expire** — urgency by design. Caps are
   config, tuned constantly.

Two structural notes: **DramaBox adding programmatic inventory** means the ad leg is maturing
from "rewarded video only" into a real advertising business; and **GoodShort's web top-up
discount** shows the web is the store-fee-free payment lane the category quietly relies on.

## 4 — The standard product surface (ReelShort/NetShort pattern)

- **Home**: hero banner carousel → horizontal shelves (New Release, Top 10 with rank badges,
  themed/trope shelves, originals shelf) → vertical poster cards with badges (Hot/New/Trending).
- **Tags/tropes as first-class navigation** (GoodShort's tag pages; ReelShort's trope labels on
  every card). Viewers browse by *flavor*, not just genre.
- **A show page per series**: cover, synopsis, tag chips, episode count, episode grid with
  lock states, follow/add-to-list, share, related shows.
- **For You feed**: full-screen swipe discovery (the TikTok surface) alongside the shelf home.
- **Rewards center** as a top-level tab. **My List / Following** as a top-level tab.
- **Player**: vertical, gapless, tap-hold, episode drawer, playback speed, quality selection,
  captions/subtitles; offline download as a VIP perk.
- **Community/fandom layer** (ReelShort): cast-adjacent content ("ReelTalk" behind-the-scenes
  shelf), fandom pages — the cast become creators; the platform becomes a destination, not a
  vending machine.
- **Multi-language** from early (subs first, ENG-dub shelves later).
- **Cadence**: daily new-episode drops; series of 40–100 episodes at 1–3 minutes.

## 5 — Where the incumbents are weak (the openings)

1. **Genre monoculture.** The catalog is wall-to-wall billionaire/werewolf/revenge romance
   aimed at one demographic. Comedy, docs, and male-skewing genres are acknowledged gaps that
   the newest entrants are racing to fill — none owns them yet.
2. **UA-spend addiction.** ReelShort loses money at $1.2B gross because acquisition is
   brute-force ad buying. Nobody attributes *which hook produces payers* with science; they
   attribute with budget. (This is JVScope's exact competence.)
3. **Wall friction as a complaint engine.** Store reviews across all majors repeat the same
   wounds: charged for episodes already seen, ad-stuffed free paths, confusing coin math.
   A wall that is honest, predictable, and never double-charges is a *retention* feature —
   and our ledger makes it provable.
4. **Closed shops.** The majors are studios with apps. None offers infrastructure to other
   producers. A multi-tenant platform with audited to-the-penny books is a structural
   difference no incumbent can bolt on.

## 6 — What this validates and what it corrects in binjreel's plan

**Validated:** IAP-primary economics; coins + subscription dual rail; free-episodes-then-wall;
1–2 minute vertical episodes; social-clip top-of-funnel; owning the money core as the IP;
comedy/all-genre positioning (the industry's own next bet); the account-bound never-lose-a-
purchase promise (the category's loudest complaint, inverted into our promise).

**Corrected (gaps in our v1 design):** no free-earning economy (the third leg); no browse layer
(shelves, tags, search, show pages, lists); no For You feed; no rewards center; no ad-network
seam; no web top-up lane; no VIP perks beyond access (HD/offline/ad-free); no community/fandom
layer; no localization plan. Resolved in PLATFORM-DESIGN.md and BUILD-PLAN-v2.md.
